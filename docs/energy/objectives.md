# Energy, cost, peak demand and emissions

Declare the objective and measurement boundary before optimizing.

| Objective | Evidence needed | Why another objective may not improve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy reduction, kWh | Comparable totals and operating conditions | Savings outside the peak window may leave peak demand unchanged. |
| Cost reduction, currency | Actual tariff, contract, billing period and metered quantities | Shifting the same energy to cheaper hours can lower cost without saving energy. |
| Peak reduction, kW | Demand window, aligned loads and relevant period | Spreading consumption can lower a peak while leaving energy unchanged. |
| Emissions reduction, mass of specified gas/CO2e | Appropriate emissions method, factor and counterfactual | Fewer kWh alone does not quantify avoided emissions. |

## Tariffs

Tariffs may include energy charges, time-of-use prices, demand charges, fixed fees, taxes, minimums or other components. Demand may depend on specified windows or earlier billing periods. Export compensation may differ from import pricing. Use only documented applicable components and effective dates; do not invent a flat rate or assume a local tariff structure.

## Emissions

Match factor geography, year, temporal resolution, units and accounting boundary. Distinguish CO2 from CO2e and footprint from avoided-emission factors. Average factors do not automatically represent marginal effects. Imports and exports may need separate treatment.

If tariffs or emissions factors are unavailable, report supported physical quantities and list the missing inputs. No Kazakhstan-specific rules or factors are assumed.
